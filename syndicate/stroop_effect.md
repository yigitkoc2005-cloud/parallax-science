# Why Does the Stroop Effect Slow You Down? (Try 3 Rounds)

**Value-first explanation (for Reddit / Quora / Dev.to / Substack):**

You see RED, but your answer should be blue. Before these 3 rounds end, can you stop blaming 1 word? Round one: name this patch's color aloud. Easy? Keep that answer ready; the next round changes something. Round two: name the ink, ignoring the word. Predict whether you'll hesitate. Don't change the rule. If you stumbled, would you blame yourself, or accuse the test of cheating? Choose your culprit. But here's the strange part: reading the word usually suffers much less from conflicting ink. Why the imbalance? Researchers debate the explanation. Reading habits matter, but automatic reading alone isn't a complete account. Round three reverses your job: read the word instead. Same conflict; a different demand. The ink is blue; the word is RED. Competing information disrupts color naming more than word reading. A hesitation needn't mean you failed. You saw the ink; the word challenged your control.

**Sources:**
- Stroop, J. R. (1935). Studies of interference in serial verbal reactions. Journal of Experimental Psychology, 18(6), 643–662. https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Stroop/ — Primary evidence for stronger interference from conflicting words during color naming than from conflicting ink during word reading; practice effects constrain an absolute loss-of-control interpretation.
- MacLeod, C. M. (1991). Half a century of research on the Stroop effect: An integrative review. Psychological Bulletin, 109(2), 163–203. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2034749/ — Reviews the interference asymmetry, automaticity, practice and competing explanations; neither simple relative-speed nor automatic-reading explanations fully account for the findings. https://melaniestefan.net/MacLeod1991.pdf
- MacLeod, C. M., & MacDonald, P. A. (2000). Interdimensional interference in the Stroop effect: Uncovering the cognitive and neural anatomy of attention. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 4(10), 383–391. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11025281/ — Additional support for interference between relevant and irrelevant dimensions and the role of relative automaticity. The three rounds are demonstrations, not measured individual results; no viewer delay or accuracy is asserted.
