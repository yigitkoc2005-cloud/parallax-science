# Why Can Roman Concrete Heal Cracks? (Inside the 2023 Test)

**Value-first explanation (for Reddit / Quora / Dev.to / Substack):**

You risk discarding Roman concrete's repair material as waste. But why did MIT's 2023 mix stop your 0.5-millimeter leak? The Pantheon's dome is nearly two thousand years old. It remains the world's largest unreinforced concrete dome. This study examined mortar from Privernum, not the Pantheon. White lumps inside the samples looked like manufacturing mistakes. Researchers had blamed such lumps on poor mixing. The team investigated whether they were evidence of something else. But here's the strange part: a crack could expose its own repair supply. Those white lumps were lime clasts. The study linked them to hot mixing with quicklime. Adding water releases heat, leaving reactive calcium reserves. Later, water entering a crack can dissolve calcium from exposed clasts. Calcium carbonate then crystallizes inside the gap. In Roman-inspired laboratory samples, cracks around half a millimeter wide sealed against water flow. Clast-free controls kept leaking. The test demonstrated sealing; it didn't establish restored structural strength. Nor does it settle every Roman recipe. The experiment reframes the flaw: what looks like waste could be Roman concrete's repair material.
