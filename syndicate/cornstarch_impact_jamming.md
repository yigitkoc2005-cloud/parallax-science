# Why Does Cornstarch Feel Solid When You Hit It?

**Value-first explanation (for Reddit / Quora / Dev.to / Substack):**

Your fingers stir this kitchen goo easily, but your foot may meet resistance. Why? Everyone says stuff is solid or liquid. This goo ignores the rule. Press slowly, and your foot sinks like soup. Hit it fast, and it feels like a floor. Inside, starch grains drift in water, sliding past each other like a calm crowd. But here's the strange part: your hit builds the wall. Grains get shoved together, and a jammed patch races outward in thousandths of a second. Picture a panicked crowd at one doorway. Nobody can move, so the mass pushes back. Scientists still debate how much comes from jammed grain chains and how much from other stresses. Stop moving and the wall melts back to liquid. Nothing is trapping you. Next time your fingers stir kitchen goo, hit it fast. You will notice it tonight.

**Sources:**
- Waitukaitis & Jaeger, 2012, Impact-activated solidification of dense suspensions via dynamic jamming fronts, Nature 487:205-209 (mechanism of jamming front; contribution of other stresses is still debated)
- Brown & Jaeger, 2014, Shear thickening in concentrated suspensions: phenomenology, mechanisms and relations to jamming, Reports on Progress in Physics 77:046602 (several mechanisms compete; no single settled answer)
