# Why Does Spaghetti Keep Snapping After the First Crack?

**Value-first explanation (for Reddit / Quora / Dev.to / Substack):**

Your dry spaghetti breaks in your hands. But why doesn't the first crack finish the job? Bending stores elastic energy. A crack launches a fast bending wave toward both ends. The wave briefly bends each fragment again. That extra bend can focus stress at a weak spot before the first piece settles. But here's the strange part: the first crack starts a cascade. Each wave reshapes the strand, so the next break depends on its bend at that instant. A snap sends a shiver down the rod. Where motion meets a flaw, the local bend can spike and trigger another fracture. Twisting changes the race. A twist wave can travel faster than a bending wave, reducing extra bending that drives repeated cracks. Results depend on setup. Flaws and bending conditions still matter, so results vary. Next time spaghetti cracks, watch the remaining strand: it may not be finished.
