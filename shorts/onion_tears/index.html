Question: Why Do Onions Make You Cry? (The Missing Enzyme)?

Your onion tears hid 1 enzyme's role until 2002. What did scientists miss before learning how to stop your tears? The sting starts with broken onion cells. Chemicals that were kept apart can finally mix. An enzyme called alliinase breaks down sulfur compounds, producing a reactive ingredient called sulfenic acid. Scientists thought this ingredient became the tear-maker spontaneously. They knew the starting material and the result. The step between them looked like it needed no enzyme. But here's the strange part: that ingredient has another destination. Making tears is only one chemical route. Lachrymatory-factor synthase, identified in 2002, redirects sulfenic acid into the tear-triggering chemical. That was the missing enzyme. That chemical evaporates, reaches your eyes, and irritates them. The result is the familiar flood of tears. In 2008, researchers silenced the enzyme's gene. Experimental onions produced far less of the eye irritant. More precursor went into other sulfur compounds. The chemistry was rerouted, not erased. Your onion tears depended on a chemical detour. Scientists had missed the enzyme at the junction.
