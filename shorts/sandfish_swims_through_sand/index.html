Question: Sand Looks Solid. So How Does This Lizard Swim?

Your fingers make ripples in a bowl. But one wave travels through a buried lizard. Why doesn't sand stop it? Look at your thumb. Press it into your palm. Feel that firm push? Sand pushes back too, so legs would just plow. But the sandfish pins its legs to its sides. Remember one number: one fifth. X-rays caught it. A bend runs head to tail, therefore each curve shoves grains back, and grains shove the body forward. But here's the strange part: that fifth is how tall each bend stands beside its length. Any deeper wastes effort, models suggest. Scientists still debate details, because the best model depends on how tightly the grains are packed. So sand isn't a wall. Pushed just right, it flows like a thick fluid. Your ripple, buried. Same wave. Different ocean. Your bowl ripples; the desert ripples back.
