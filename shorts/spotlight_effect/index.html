Question: Do People Notice Your Embarrassment? (The T-Shirt Test)?

You risk hiding after 1 blush and 1 glance. But before you blame them, what did researchers check? Replay that awkward moment. Your face burns; surely the room registered it. Predict: half the room, or fewer? In a 2000 experiment, students wore a potentially embarrassing Barry Manilow T-shirt into a room. They expected roughly half the observers to identify him. Would you bet lower? But here's the strange part: feeling exposed wasn't evidence that anyone remembered. The uncomfortable possibility: your embarrassment can inflate your estimate of other people's attention. Researchers call this the spotlight effect. Not proof people never notice or judge you. They checked observers' recall against wearers' predictions. What survived outside the wearer's head? Only about 23 percent identified the singer: roughly half the predicted audience. That glance: evidence they noticed, or your own spotlight? Which would you bet on now?
