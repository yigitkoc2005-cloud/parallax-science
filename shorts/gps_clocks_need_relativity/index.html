Question: Why Your Phone's GPS Needs a Slow Clock?

Your phone's map is quietly rescued from a clock problem you can't see. Can you guess how? GPS finds you by timing satellite signals. A clock error becomes a distance error. Two effects pull opposite ways. Weaker gravity makes satellite clocks tick faster. Their speed slows them down. But here's the strange part: gravity wins. Together, satellite clocks gain about 38 microseconds each day—enough to create roughly eleven kilometers of range error. The fix starts before launch: satellite clocks are preset to tick slow. Your receiver corrects smaller shifts as the orbit changes. Think of runners sending timed flashes. A racing watch makes each flash seem farther away. Your phone compares satellite signal times to find your position. So who gets blamed: your map or the clock in the sky? Would you trust Einstein with your directions, or call it a clock problem?
